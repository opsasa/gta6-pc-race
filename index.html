What the estimate rests on

  • One project went from nothing public to loading the game world in a day, and XCL took lighter retail games from first boot to playable in a day or two. No project has done that with a game of this size.
  • The in-game gate fell on 24 November, a day after the executable appeared. By that evening a public fork, now named victory-fork, had the first gameplay scene on screen under player control, and others have since run it on their own machines, on NVIDIA and AMD cards.
  • Most of the picture is there, and the game itself holds up. The fork lights its first room and shows characters, textures, sky and sunlit surfaces with shaded areas still too dark, AnyXboxX reports every cutscene playing in full, and builds of the fork's code report the first two missions played through.
  • Stability now has three reports beyond the length the bar asks for: 30 minutes on an RTX 4090 in an open pull request, about 45 minutes through two missions relayed by AnyXboxX, and more than 90 minutes on an RTX 3050 in ztrx's README. Speed is the remaining job. The best figure from a long run is about 7.6 frames a second, with most cards at 3 to 7, up from under 1 for the best public code on the morning of 24 November. On 25 November victory-fork merged its first frame-rate work without a gain in frame time on its own test machine, VItoriaBr reported its figure rising from about 3 to as much as 6 on an RTX 5060 Ti after taking that work, and a new fork, victory-perf, reported 9 to 14 in interior scenes at reduced resolution on one RTX 4070 SUPER, which nobody else has confirmed. The only other reduced-resolution measurement, on an RTX 3090 Ti, is 6.1 to 6.4. XCL, a separate code base, reports about 5 in its own 3D world on an RTX 5090, with no long runs. Playable needs 20.

Its argument is not specific to Leonida, and no other listed project has been named so far, though one has since removed the game's name from its code and another has republished with a new history. On 25 November two outlets reported, from a reporter's post, that Take-Two is also sending notices over social media posts that show footage of unofficial builds. A notice cannot reach private builds, but it can end the open collaboration the public ones rely on.

Method and limits

  • Playable builds

    None exist. Any download that claims to be GTA VI for PC is fake, and fake builds carrying malware have circulated since August.

  • The build

    These projects work from a leaked Xbox build. Rockstar has not released or announced a PC version. This page does not link to the leak or explain how to get or run it.

  • Sources

    Each project's public repository and open pull requests and, where marked, its chat. Project names link to those repositories to credit the work.

  • Stage claims

    Every stage is the project's own claim unless its source line says otherwise, and most are unverified. CGBJ's comes from press reports, because its code is private.

  • Crash reports

    By one project's notes, the game's crash reporter sends data to an outside server each time it crashes.

  • Who compiles it

    An AI assistant (Claude), automatically, about every 15 minutes. It can be wrong.

  • Affiliation

    None with Rockstar Games, Take-Two Interactive, Microsoft or any project listed.
